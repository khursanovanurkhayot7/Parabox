# Parabox Campaign Progression Audit

## Result

The campaign is now an intentional 50-level curriculum rather than five disconnected difficulty ramps. Every authored solution replays successfully through the real puzzle model, every level's measured design evidence is greater than the previous level's, no adjacent increase exceeds 21%, and all introduced mechanics return on a later practice or mastery board.

Difficulty is created with route planning, commitment, dependencies, state interactions, player-body assignment, and inner/outer prediction. The pass does not enlarge boards to manufacture difficulty, add random objects, or add visual clutter.

## What Was Wrong

- The old order treated level number as difficulty evidence. That hid genuine inversions.
- Several short or nearly automatic routes appeared after puzzles with longer dependency chains.
- Chapter boundaries reset the challenge instead of continuing it, especially Levels 30 to 31 and Levels 40 to 41.
- A number of mechanics appeared once and disappeared, so their first level felt arbitrary rather than educational.
- The first multi-player-body level was too short and disconnected from the skills mastered in Chapter IV.
- Some late puzzles increased symbol count without increasing meaningful decisions.

## Redesign Rules

Each chapter uses **Teach → Practice → Experiment → Combine → Twist → Mastery**. A new rule first appears in a readable setting, returns later in a harder context, and is eventually combined with another learned rule. New challenge must add a meaningful decision or a dependency, not decoration.

The difficulty evidence stored on every level is computed from its authored route length, direction changes, movable objects, targets, distinct mechanics, linked dependencies, mechanic tiles, player bodies, and nested state. It contains no level-number bonus. The release validator rejects:

- a level whose evidence does not exceed the preceding level;
- an adjacent evidence jump greater than 21%;
- a chapter whose minimum or average evidence does not exceed the preceding chapter;
- an invalid or over-limit stored solution;
- an unannounced first mechanic appearance;
- a mechanic that never receives a later authored rehearsal;
- duplicate boards, missing targets, excessive clutter, or a broken finale room structure.
